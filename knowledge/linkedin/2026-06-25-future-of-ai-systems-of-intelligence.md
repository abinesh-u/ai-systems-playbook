Over the past few months, as I've been building and studying multi-agent systems, I've started to realize something:

The future of AI won't be defined by individual agents.
It will be defined by how intelligence is organized.

Most discussions around Agentic AI focus on the capabilities of a single agent:
• Better reasoning
• Better tool usage
• Better planning
• Better models

Those improvements matter.
But they aren't the problem I'm most interested in.

The real challenge is coordination.

Recently, I explored OpenAI's workflow architecture built with Codex CLI, MCP, and the Agents SDK.

What caught my attention wasn't the technology stack.
It was the organizational design.

A Project Manager Agent orchestrates the workflow.
Specialized agents handle design, development, testing, and implementation.
Shared artifacts maintain context across the entire system.
Responsibilities are clearly defined.
Communication follows structured paths.
Knowledge is persisted instead of repeatedly rediscovered.

When I looked at the architecture, it didn't feel like software.
It felt like an organization.

And that observation changed the way I think about AI systems.

Every successful organization depends on:
• Defined roles
• Clear ownership
• Shared knowledge
• Communication protocols
• Governance mechanisms
• Feedback loops

The same principles are now emerging inside multi-agent architectures.

Which raises an interesting question:
What if the next generation of AI engineering is less about building smarter agents and more about designing better intelligence systems?

As AI systems grow in complexity, I believe engineers will spend less time writing prompts and more time designing:
→ Agent orchestration layers
→ Memory architectures
→ Coordination mechanisms
→ Context-sharing strategies
→ Reliability and governance frameworks
→ Intelligence infrastructure

Because I don't think we're just building AI applications anymore.
We're designing systems of intelligence.

And the engineers who understand system design, orchestration, and organizational thinking will be the ones shaping what comes next.

This is the direction I'm currently exploring:
• Multi-Agent Systems
• Agent Orchestration
• AI System Design
• Intelligence Infrastructure
