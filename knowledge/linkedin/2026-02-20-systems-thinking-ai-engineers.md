I used to think building a good AI system meant building a good model.
Most people don't fail in AI because they don't know models.
They fail because they don't think in end-to-end systems.

You can train a model with great accuracy.
But if you can't answer these, it won't survive production:

• Where does the data come from — and how does it break?
• How do you version datasets and models?
• What happens when the distribution shifts?
• How do you monitor, roll back, and explain predictions?

Accuracy is a metric.
Reliability is a mindset.

Lately, I've been deliberately shifting my focus from "build a model" to
"design a system" — pipelines, infra, evaluation, failure modes, and trade-offs.

That's what real-world AI actually demands.

If you're early in AI/ML:
Learn models — but obsess over how they live in the real world.
That's where engineers are separated from notebook heroes.
